Warrant Article 22: Resolution Supporting Modernization of Proposition 2 1/2

A non-binding measure for the May 26, 2026 Annual Town Meeting asking Brookline to support state-level modernization of Proposition 2 1/2.

Article 22 would place Brookline Town Meeting on record in favor of modernizing Proposition 2 1/2, the state law that generally limits annual property tax levy growth to 2.5 percent plus new growth.

The draft article is submitted by Rob Schoen and Bradford Kimball, both identified in the warrant as Town Meeting Members from Precinct 1.

The resolution does not change Brookline's levy limit, approve an override, or endorse a single reform proposal. Instead, it asks the Governor and General Court to address what the article describes as the structural limits of the current fixed nominal cap while preserving taxpayer protections.

The text lays out four main arguments: that the 2.5 percent cap is fixed in nominal terms, that municipal purchasing power can erode when costs rise faster than that rate, that repeated override campaigns can create uncertainty and voter fatigue, and that the Massachusetts Municipal Association has already proposed voter-driven reforms such as inflation-indexed levy growth and multi-year overrides.

The petitioner description attached to the article frames it as both a policy signal and a conversation starter. It highlights examples of possible reform, but the warrant article itself stays broad and does not ask Town Meeting to back any one legislative model.

If adopted, Article 22 would do three concrete things:

  • Formally recommend modernization of Proposition 2 1/2
  • Call on the Governor and Legislature to pass reform legislation
  • Direct the Town Clerk to send the resolution to state officials and the MMA

It would not do the following on its own:

  • Raise property taxes
  • Change Brookline's current override question
  • Commit the Town to a specific reform such as inflation indexing
  • Create any immediate budget relief for FY2027

The broader backdrop is a statewide debate over whether Proposition 2 1/2 still matches current municipal cost pressures. Earlier posts on this site summarized the MMA's A Perfect Storm report and its Navigating the Storm follow-up, both of which argue that municipalities are facing a structural mismatch between rising costs and constrained revenue growth. This site has also posted a counterpoint from Tax Foundation, Massachusetts Proposition 2 1/2 Is Working.
