Final Report from the Expenditures & Revenues Study Committee (E&RSC)

The committee's final report was released on March 13, 2026.

Brookline's Expenditures & Revenues Study Committee has now released its final report on the town's fiscal condition and recommendations for the Select Board as override discussions continue.

Note: the local PDF embedded below has the appendix removed. For the complete report, including the appendix, use the full file from the Town of Brookline.

AI-generated summary of the report:

The E&RSC final report argues that Brookline has a long-running structural deficit: revenues, especially property taxes constrained by Proposition 2 1/2, are growing more slowly than major expenditures such as wages, health insurance, pensions, debt service, inflation, and special education. The committee estimates a three-year gap of about $7.95 million for Town operations and $17.94 million for the Public Schools of Brookline (PSB). It concludes that Brookline cannot sustain current service levels without either significant new revenue or deep service cuts.

The full committee recommends putting an operating override on the May 2026 ballot, but strongly favors giving voters more than an all-or-nothing choice. It reviews three approaches: a full $23.25 million override, a front-loaded $18.6 million version that uses reserves to smooth later years, and a tiered ballot structure that lets voters choose among funding levels. The report's preferred approach is the tiered option, because it believes a neutral ballot with multiple choices is more likely to pass and better reflects taxpayer concerns. The report also emphasizes that any override should be paired with clearer long-term cost controls and stronger collaboration between the Town and schools.

The Schools Subcommittee supports an override only if Brookline adopts a formal Town-School commitment to improve PSB's cost structure and rebuild public confidence. Its recommendations include reducing the Town's unusually generous health insurance contribution over time, addressing the impact of salary steps, evaluating school and middle-school models, improving financial systems and controls, enforcing teacher workload provisions, charging more appropriately for school-space rentals, and reviewing BEEP tuition so it moves closer to full cost recovery. It also recommends a follow-up review of prior CLA findings on financial controls and management practices.

The Town Subcommittee concludes that a $5.31 million override is needed to preserve municipal services, fund contractual obligations, address chronic fire overtime, restore police positions, support tree and sustainability work, maintain senior transportation, and stabilize future budgets. Without an override, it warns of position cuts, reduced service levels, and possible fire company closures.

The Future Subcommittee focuses on longer-term fixes. It recommends a solar investment program and electric demand-management strategies to reduce energy costs, a study of school consolidation or revised grade configurations to improve efficiency, a more assertive economic development strategy to expand the tax base, and a future review of OPEB funding policy. Across all sections, the report stresses the tradeoff Brookline faces: higher taxes and affordability pressures on residents versus major reductions in town and school services if new revenue is rejected.
