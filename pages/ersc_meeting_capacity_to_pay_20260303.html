E&RSC Meeting Recap (March 3, 2026): Capacity to Pay and Non-Resident Special Education

Recap of the Expenditures & Revenues Study Committee meeting topics and supporting analysis for March 3, 2026.

The March 3 E&RSC meeting focused on two major topics: capacity to pay in relation to a proposed operating override, and non-resident special education costs discussed through the School Subcommittee item.

During discussion of override size, committee materials presented a combined request of about $23.8 million, including $5.31 million from town departments and $18.54 million from the public schools. The meeting references projected property tax impacts of 8.3% in FY27 and 19.3% over FY26-FY29 when combined with ongoing Proposition 2 1/2 growth, new growth assumptions, debt exclusions, and the CPA surcharge.

The meeting also addressed historical override timing and current debt context, including major school construction and renovation borrowing. A recurring theme was how to balance service funding needs against cumulative tax pressure on residents.

Peer-community and inflation comparisons were reviewed in the meeting materials, including dollar and percentage changes in Brookline property taxes since FY15. Those comparisons were used to frame affordability and pace-of-growth questions tied to any new override amount.

Housing burden data for owners and renters, including senior households, was part of the capacity-to-pay discussion. The meeting materials also note that property tax increases can be passed through to renters under common lease structures.

Options discussed for reducing near-term override size included phasing over multiple years, health insurance contribution changes, lower wage-growth assumptions, and potential staffing/workload adjustments. Additional operational savings and revenue ideas were also presented, along with lower-cost and higher-cost override structures.

The School Subcommittee item covered non-resident special education, including METCO and Materials Fee students. Meeting materials estimate annual unreimbursed in-district costs above $4 million and discuss limited reimbursement from sending districts and the state, with recommendations for further cost analysis and state-level advocacy.

Across both major agenda topics, the meeting framing emphasizes two risks to weigh: resident burden from higher taxes and the possibility that a larger override could fail at the ballot.
