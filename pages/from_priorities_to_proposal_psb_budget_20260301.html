From Priorities to Proposal: Understanding How the PSB Budget is Built

On March 1, 2026, the Brookline Parents Organization interviewed Superintendent Bella Wong about how the PSB budget is built.

This page recaps the March 1, 2026 Brookline Parents Organization interview with Superintendent Bella Wong about how the Public Schools of Brookline develops its annual budget.

The conversation focused on budget structure, recurring cost pressures, enrollment and staffing, special education obligations, the annual planning timeline, and community questions about fiscal trade-offs.

Wong described the main PSB funding sources as town appropriations, state aid, grants and revolving funds, and program fees. She also noted that personnel costs represent the largest share of spending in the school budget.

Recurring cost pressures discussed in the interview included:

  • Contractual salary growth and step increases
  • Health insurance and benefits growth
  • Inflation in transportation, supplies, and services
  • Special education service requirements and related transportation
  • Year-to-year variability in out-of-district placement costs

The interview addressed how enrollment changes can affect staffing allocations, class size targets, and personnel planning across schools.

Special education was presented as a major cost area due to legal requirements, IEP-driven service obligations, placement decisions, and transportation needs.

Wong described a multi-step process that includes internal planning, proposal development, School Committee review, coordination with town financial planning, and final approvals.

Community Q&A topics included:

  • Budget transparency
  • Staffing decisions and program priorities
  • Special education spending patterns
  • Implications when projected costs outpace projected revenues
